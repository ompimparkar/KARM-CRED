## 1. Model details

| | |
|---|---|
| Model | `lightgbm.LGBMRegressor` (100 trees, lr 0.05, max_depth 5, seed 42) |
| Task | Regression: **TrustScore ∈ [300, 900]** (integers) |
| Training data | `backend/data/karm_cred_synthetic_data.csv` — 5,002 rows (5,000 simulated workers + 2 twin fixtures), generated by `ml/generate_data.py` |
| Split | 80/20, `random_state=42` |
| **Test R² / RMSE** | **0.6595 / 67.4 points** |
| Explainability | `shap.TreeExplainer` → per-feature impact in score points, mapped to plain-language labels |
| Artifacts | `ml/saved_models/lgbm_model.pkl`, `ml/saved_models/imputer.json` |

### Why R² ≈ 0.66 is the healthy answer here

The label is **not** a formula over the input columns. Each worker's
`trust_score` is derived from **latent roots** the model never sees
(income-floor resilience, true trend, punctuality, engagement, gap
discipline, excess volatility) plus independent Gaussian noise (σ = 0.08
capacity units ≈ ±60 score points). Observed features are noisy, measured
proxies of those roots (e.g. `income_floor_ratio` correlates with the floor
root at r ≈ +0.86, not 1.0). Consequently:

* R² ≈ **0.6–0.85 is expected and desirable** — the target contains real,
  unlearnable-from-features variance.
* An R² ≈ 0.99 would mean the label is a recombination of the inputs
  (circular evaluation). That is precisely what this dataset replaces.

| 8 | `platform_tenure_months` | Months active across gig platforms | simulated | 2 – 60 (≈25) |
| 9 | `avg_savings_ratio` | Monthly savings ÷ income | simulated | 0.02 – 0.40 (≈0.16) |
| 10 | `avg_platform_rating` | Average platform stars (1.0–5.0) | simulated; **nullable** (source `platform`) | 1.6 – 5.0 (3.45) |

**Explicitly excluded:** `raw_income_cv` (flat volatility scalar). It exists in
the CSV as a **diagnostic column only** and is never a model input.

**Cohort columns** (`gig_type`, `city`, `vehicle_class`) are not model
inputs; they are used for cohort-median imputation and for
cohort-adjustment of volatility.

## 3. Label construction

```
capacity = 0.30·floor_root            # rewards a strong income floor
         + 0.20·clip(trend/0.012, ±1) # penalizes sustained decline
         + 0.25·punctuality           # on-time payment behaviour
         + 0.15·engagement            # savings / tenure / activity
         + 0.10·gap_root              # penalizes erratic payday gaps
         − 0.04·clip(excess_vol/0.12) # MILD penalty: swings unusual for cohort
         + N(0, 0.08)                 # independent label noise
trust_score = clip(300 + 600·(capacity − 0.10)/0.80, 300, 900)
```

* **Never penalized:** raw variability, cohort-shared seasonality
  (festival spikes / monsoon dips / weekend lumpiness).
* The excess-volatility penalty is deliberately mild (max ≈ −30 points);
  `fairness_check.py` proves the model's realized penalty for
  otherwise-healthy high-volatility workers stays within tolerance.
* Distribution: mean 558, σ 120, p10 394 / p50 563 / p90 711.

## 4. Missing-data policy (cold start)

~27% of rows are partial profiles (renters with no utility bill, cash-paid
workers with no UPI history, new workers with no linked platform profile).
Policy, implemented in `ml/data_utils.py`:

1. Missing value → **cohort median** (same gig/city/vehicle from the
   training split), falling back to **training-set global median** when the
   cohort value or cohort itself is unknown (true cold start).
2. `data_completeness` = min(provided, present/3) travels with every
   request — the model can damp reliance on imputed evidence and the UI
   stays honest about a thin file.
3. Imputer statistics are **fit on the training split only** and persisted
   to `imputer.json`; serving loads the file (no test-time leakage).
4. Consent-denied sources are nulled **before** imputation, so completeness
   reflects the withdrawal too.

## 5. Fairness check (results)

Question: does the model systematically score *high cohort-adjusted
volatility* workers lower when everything else looks healthy
(floor ≥ 0.25, trend ≥ 0, utility on-time ≥ 0.70)?

Eval set (1,001 rows) bucketed by `cohort_adjusted_volatility` decile:

| Decile | vol range | n | mean pred (all) | n healthy | mean pred (healthy) | mean actual (healthy) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | [−0.134, −0.074] | 101 | 614.1 | 43 | 631.8 | 627.1 |
| D2 | [−0.074, −0.052] | 100 | 604.4 | 35 | 628.8 | 637.0 |
| D3 | [−0.052, −0.031] | 100 | 594.1 | 39 | 624.7 | 619.2 |
| D4 | [−0.031, −0.015] | 100 | 576.0 | 28 | 632.4 | 622.8 |
| D5 | [−0.015, +0.000] | 100 | 569.5 | 37 | 629.7 | 629.7 |
| D6 | [+0.000, +0.018] | 100 | 557.3 | 36 | 628.4 | 622.6 |
| D7 | [+0.018, +0.035] | 100 | 518.7 | 15 | 659.2 | 648.1 |
| D8 | [+0.036, +0.062] | 100 | 548.4 | 21 | 666.4 | 674.4 |
| D9 | [+0.062, +0.096] | 100 | 514.8 | 12 | 661.1 | 613.7 |
| D10 | [+0.096, +0.198] | 100 | 479.8 | 8 | 681.3 | 668.4 |

**Healthy D10 − D1 gap = +49.5 points** (tolerance ≥ −30) → **PASS**.
Otherwise-healthy workers with unusual volatility score *above* their
smooth-cohort peers — they combine unusual swings with a strong floor and
reliable payments — the opposite of a flat volatility penalty. All-worker
means decline across deciles (−134 points), reflecting the label's small,
legitimate excess-volatility penalty plus the (observed) tilt of high-swing
workers toward thinner floors; the healthy-subgroup rows isolate the bias
the problem statement warns about.

## 7. Limitations & out-of-scope

* **Synthetic data.** All conclusions hold for the simulator's assumptions;
  real gig-income traces would be needed before any production claim.
* **Consent sources are simulated.** The app demo simulates on-device SMS
  parsing; the production path for UPI/bank data is the Account Aggregator
  framework (see README, Privacy & compliance), which is not implemented.
* **Cohort definition** (gig × city × vehicle) is a heuristic; poorly
  populated cohorts fall back to global medians.
* **Missingness is informative in reality** (not MCAR as simulated):
  renters differ systematically from owners — partially mitigated, not
  solved, by `data_completeness`.
* **Ratings, tenure and savings are simulated as available** only through
  the same three sources; additional real sources would change the
  completeness semantics.
* **Not for real lending decisions.** No regulator-grade validation,
  adversarial testing, or drift monitoring has been performed.
