            What this page proves

            Bucket every worker into deciles of swings-vs-peers (cohort_adjusted_volatility — seasonality for their gig/city already removed) and the red line (all workers) falls with volatility — because unusual swings often do travel with genuinely risky patterns (thin income floor, shrinking earnings). That is the model doing its job: penalising risk signals.

            The green line keeps only otherwise-healthy workers (floor ≥ 25% of a typical week, non-negative trend, utility on-time ≥ 70% — the same mask the training-time fairness gate enforces). Their average score does not fall as swings grow: the wildest decile scores — above the smoothest (tolerance ±30 pts). High variability with a healthy structure is not being punished — the claim, in one chart.

            How your data is handled

            • What's stored: in this demo, only your consent grants (browser localStorage) and the API's in-memory synthetic profiles (SQLite, wiped on restart). No real PII enters the system.
            • Derived vs. sent: pattern features (income floor, trend, swings-vs-peers, payday gaps, savings ratio) are computed from your earnings history; the raw SMS/UPI records are never forwarded by this app — only derived aggregates reach the API.
            • Enforcement, not decoration: a denied source's features are set to null inside the API before the model runs (see backend/app.py consent gate); the model literally never receives them — it falls back to cohort-median imputation and reports lower data completeness.
            • Retention: demo data lives only for the server process; kill the Flask process and it's gone. A production build would keep consent-scoped records only for the purpose granted.

            Production path (beyond this demo)

            • Account Aggregator (AA) framework: RBI-regulated, consent-based financial data sharing through FIPs/FUs — grants would be purpose-limited, time-boxed and revocable at the AA layer, instead of localStorage.
            • RBI digital lending guidelines (2022): explicit borrower consent for data use, data minimisation, and traceability of lending-service partners.
            • Algorithmic transparency & fairness: reason codes handed to the applicant (the SHAP cards), periodic bias audits (the Fairness page's decile test), and a human grievance path — required in spirit by the guidelines and by fair-lending norms.
            • DPDP Act 2023: notice + consent per purpose, withdrawal as easy as grant, erasure on demand, and data-fiduciary accountability for automated decisions affecting individuals.
